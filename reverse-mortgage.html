Question · Reverse mortgage

What happens to a reverse mortgage when a parent dies in Folsom?

By Scott Kirk, Broker/Owner · Updated November 5, 2026

Short answer

The loan comes due. The family can pay it off and keep the house, sell the house, or sign it over to the lender. If the house is worth less than the loan, HUD lets the estate sell for at least 95% of the current appraised value, and the lender takes the sale proceeds as payment in full. Call the loan servicer in the first 30 days. The lender's clock usually runs faster than probate.

What are the family's choices?

There are three. Which one fits depends on whether the house is worth more or less than the loan balance.

ChoiceWhat it takes
Keep the housePay the full loan balance, usually with cash or a new loan
Sell the houseRepay the loan from the sale. Anything left goes to the estate. If the loan is more than the house is worth, sell for at least 95% of the current appraised value
Sign it over to the lenderA deed in lieu of foreclosure. The family walks away from the house and the loan

Sources: HUD, Inheriting a Home Secured by an FHA-insured HECM; Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, reverse mortgage heirs.

How long does the family have?

About 30 days to start. HUD says the loan must be handled within 30 days, and the CFPB says the family has 30 days after the lender's due and payable notice to buy, sell or sign the house over. The lender can extend that. HUD describes 90-day extensions when the family shows it is working on a sale or a loan, and the CFPB says the time may be extended up to six months.

Extensions take proof. A signed listing agreement, a purchase contract or a loan application is what the servicer wants to see.

Sources: HUD; CFPB.

Why the clock matters more in a probate

Without a living trust, someone needs court authority to sell the house. In a full probate, the first hearing to appoint the executor comes 15 to 30 days after the petition is filed. So the first 30 days can pass before anyone has the power to sign a listing. That's why the call to the servicer comes first, and why your attorney files early. If the house was in a trust, the trustee can usually act sooner.

Source: Probate Code 8003. More on the paths in Does it need probate?

Who pays the taxes and insurance in the meantime?

The estate. HUD says property taxes and insurance stay the estate's job until the title transfers. Keep the house insured, keep the taxes paid, and keep it secured while it sits empty.

Source: HUD.

What if a spouse still lives there?

A spouse who signed the loan is a borrower, and the loan stays in place. A spouse who did not sign may still be able to stay under HUD's rules for non-borrowing spouses. The first step is a certification to the lender within 30 days of the last borrower's death. Call the servicer right away.

Source: HUD.

Who should I call first?

The loan servicer. The number is on the reverse mortgage statement. If you can't reach them, HUD's FHA Resource Center is at 800-225-5342. Then a California probate attorney, if there's no trust. Then get the value, because the 95% rule and every choice above start from what the house is worth. I can pull the MetroList sales around the house so you know if the lender's appraisal looks right.

These are HUD's rules for FHA-insured reverse mortgages, called HECMs. If the loan isn't a HECM, its own loan papers set the rules.

Common questions

Do heirs have to pay back more than the house is worth?

No, not on a HECM. If the estate sells for at least 95% of the appraised value, the lender takes the proceeds, and the CFPB says the mortgage insurance covers the rest.

Can we keep Mom's house if it has a reverse mortgage?

Yes, if you pay the full loan balance. Families often do it with a new mortgage in their own name. Ask the servicer for the payoff amount and an extension while the loan is in process.

Does the house have to go through probate before we sell?

Not if it was in a living trust. Then the trustee can usually sell it. Without a trust, someone usually needs court authority first. See Does it need probate?
