Does an inherited house in Folsom have to go through probate?

By Scott Kirk, Broker/Owner · Updated November 4, 2026

Short answer

Not always. A house held in a living trust is usually sold by the trustee with no probate court. Without a trust, California has a simpler court petition for a main home with a gross value up to $750,000 when the owner died on or after April 1, 2025. Your attorney confirms whether the estate qualifies; a neighborhood median can't decide it. Above that, it usually takes full probate in the superior court of the county where the person lived, which is Sacramento County for a Folsom resident, and creditors get at least four months after the executor is appointed to file claims.

Which path fits your family?

Start at the top and follow the answers. Your probate attorney confirms the path, but most Folsom families can tell where they likely land in a minute. If the house passed by joint tenancy, a transfer on death deed, or to a surviving spouse, ask your attorney first. It may skip probate.

The California limits

The courts publish the limits, and they depend on the date of death.

Date of deathSmall estate affidavit (personal property)Main home petition limit
April 1, 2025 or later$208,850$750,000, for the main home in California
April 1, 2022 to March 31, 2025$184,500$184,500 (no separate main home limit before April 1, 2025)

A house cannot pass by the small estate affidavit. Other real property of small value has its own affidavit, capped at $69,625 for a death on or after April 1, 2025. The main home petition also requires waiting 40 days after the death and an appraisal by the probate referee.

Source: California Courts, Probate Code adjusted dollar amounts.

How long does full probate take?

Longer than most families expect. A few steps have minimum times set by law.

  1. 1First hearing to appoint the executor. 15 to 30 days after the petition is filed, or 30 to 45 days if requested.
  2. 2Creditor claim period. 4 months after the executor's letters are issued, or 60 days after a known creditor is notified, whichever is later.
  3. 3Notice to heirs before a sale. At least 15 days under full authority.

These set the earliest dates for parts of the process. The creditor period limits when the estate can pay out and close, not when the house can sell. Court calendars and family questions add time. Ask your attorney for a realistic timeline before you set a sale date.

Sources: Probate Code 8003; Probate Code 9100; Probate Code 10586.
